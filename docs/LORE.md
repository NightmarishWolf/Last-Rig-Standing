# Last Rig Standing: the world

The campaign's story bible. Missions should stay consistent with this; change it here first.

## The Drawdown

By the 2050s the land fields were finished. Wells that had pumped for a century coughed up water and sand, and one by one the great onshore fields were capped. What oil was left lay under the sea, on a handful of continental shelves: deep, expensive, and finite.

The years when the world realised this are called **the Drawdown**. Fuel prices went vertical, then supply went with them. Grids failed, harvests rotted in fields with no diesel to bring them in, and coastal cities that lived on shipping and fishing emptied out. Three short wars were fought over the shelves before anyone agreed to stop.

## The Allocation Accords (2054)

The nations that could still project power at sea signed the **Allocation Accords**: every offshore field is held in common by the signatories, every barrel is metered, and each member receives a quota by formula. The Accords ended the shelf wars, and for the people inside them, they work. The lights stay on.

The quotas are enforced by the **Coalition Navy**, a joint fleet answering to the Allocation Council. It guards the rigs, escorts the tankers, and keeps everyone else off the shelves.

Everyone else is a lot of people. Nations too small or too broke to sign. Coastal towns whose governments traded them away for a bigger quota. Fishing fleets with no fuel to fish. Stateless crews. The Accords call them **the Unallocated**.

## The Free Tide

The Unallocated didn't disappear. They lashed their boats together into floating towns (rafts of hulls, decking and drums that drift with the seasons) and started taking oil where they could: siphoning rigs at night, boarding tankers, buying and selling on a black market that runs from raft to raft.

They call themselves **the Free Tide**. Their flag is a white wave on blue. To the Navy they are pirates and thieves; to themselves they are survivors taking back what was fenced off from them. Both are true.

The Tide is not one organisation. Each raft-town governs itself, and the captains who lead the largest of them co-operate when it suits them.

## Ships and technology

It is 2061. The navies of the Drawdown are lean: fuel is rationed even for the Coalition, so its fleet favours efficient hulls and long patrols over big battle groups, at least at first. The Tide sails whatever floats: converted fishing boats, skiffs, barges, and ships taken from the Navy.

## Places

- **The Corrigan Shelf.** A mid-sized Coalition field in cold northern water: a dozen rigs, one outpost (**Corrigan Point**), and a long rocky ridge called **the Spit** that cuts the shelf in two. The only passage through is **the Narrows**. Rig 7 sits past the reef on the far side.

- **The Fenwick Isles.** A ring of islands around a sheltered lagoon, far from any Navy base. Home of **Driftwood**, a raft town of some three hundred people and the base the convoy raiders sailed from.
- **The Saltpan Channel.** The tanker lane from Corrigan Point to the **Allocation Terminal**, between two chains of low islands whose coves hide Tide boats. The Navy's forward base at its western end is **Saltpan Station**. Its raft-towns (2061): **Gullhaven** and **Lantern** in the northern coves, **Tarry Reach** in the south, **Mercy** (a hospital raft where the wounded of every raft-town end up, white tarps painted with red crosses), and **the Kiln**, Kade's own base, at the far southeastern end.
- **The Drowned Reach.** A broken atoll far to the south of the shelves: open sea and scattered islands to the west, a ring of reef around a wide lagoon in the east. Inside is **Haven**, Ruiz's home raft. The way in is **the Gate** (two gun forts and a lighthouse); the back door is **the Needle**, a narrow pass to the south-east. Kade's last base, **the Cinder Isle**, is north-east of the ring.
- **Bracken Bank.** The shelf's southern fields, never properly surveyed. On its coast is **Bracken Point**, an Unallocated fishing village whose boats work the bank and trade with the Tide. The richest deposit on the bank lies under its fishing grounds.

(More places are added as missions are written.)

## Characters

### The Allocation Council

- **The Council.** The signatories' governing body; it sets the quotas and the Navy answers to it. Never seen, never named: on screen it's a blank, backlit silhouette, a single voice speaking for all of them. Introduced in Mission 7.

### Coalition Navy

- **Captain Mateo Ortiz** (commander: *Marines*). Newly promoted, on his first command. Believes in the Accords because he grew up on the right side of them: his family's town kept its lights on when the neighbours' didn't. Earnest, careful, and about to have that belief tested. The player's main viewpoint in the Navy arc.
- **Admiral Ruth Hale** (commander: *Battleline*). Commander of the northern shelves and Ortiz's superior. A veteran of the last shelf war; she remembers what the world was like before the Accords and considers them the only thing standing between civilisation and another Drawdown. Hard, fair, and certain.
- **Commodore Elias Vance** (commander: *Carrier Doctrine*). Commander of the shelf's escort group from Mission 3. Sharp, ambitious and unsentimental: he thinks guarding rigs is a losing game and the Navy should clear the sea of the Tide entirely, rafts and all. He judges Ortiz by what he did at Bracken.
- **Lt. Kenji Sato.** Ortiz's executive officer. Practical, dry, by the book; the voice that teaches the player the ropes in the first missions.

### The Unallocated

- **Maren Holt.** Elder of Bracken Point. Her family has fished the bank for four generations. Not Tide, not Navy; she wants her village left alone, and she remembers who leaves it alone.

### Free Tide

- **Lio.** A teenager from Driftwood. If Ortiz takes the town instead of burning it, Lio is among the detained, and promises that Mother Ruiz will hear about it.

- **"Mother" Ruiz** (commander: *Endless Swarm*). Leads the largest raft-town. A former harbour master whose town was traded away in the Accords. Feeds thousands and will do anything to keep doing it. First heard (on the radio only) in Mission 5, aboard her Drone Mothership; she knew every name in Driftwood.
- **Kade the Burner** (commander: *Arson*). Young, angry and dangerous; believes the rigs should burn if the Tide can't have them. Introduces himself over the radio in Mission 3, then sends a fireship at the Hesper.
- **Old Salt Nyx** (commander: *Smugglers*). Runs the black market. Sells to everyone, including, quietly, the Navy. First heard in Mission 6, selling Ortiz charts of the Saltpan raft-towns ("Everybody pays me, Captain"), and selling Kade the Navy's positions in the same breath.

## The Navy arc, Part One: The Allocation

Ten missions, all written. The arc's theme is **duty and doubt**: Ortiz begins certain he's protecting the world, and over ten missions learns what the quotas cost and who pays.

1. **First Watch** (Corrigan Shelf, March 2061). Rig 7 goes silent. Ortiz takes two Picket Boats through the Narrows and finds the rig drained, its crew gone, and the Tide's flag on the derrick. *Teaches: moving, gunnery, rounds, fog of war, contacts.*
2. **The Quota** (Bracken Bank, April 2061). Losing Rig 7 put Corrigan behind its quota. Ortiz surveys the bank, drills new rigs and sells enough oil to make up the shortfall, while a Tide skiff tries to siphon his rigs. The richest well lies under Bracken Point's fishing grounds: drill it and the village packs up and leaves for the rafts; leave it and Hale notes coldly that the Council won't thank him for his conscience, and Maren says the village won't forget. *Teaches: selling and the price, upkeep and debt, surveying, building rigs, storage, siphoning.*
3. **Convoy** (Saltpan Channel, May 2061). Commodore Vance takes over the escort group, hands Ortiz the Corrigan Point shipyard, and orders him to see the tanker *Hesper* north to the Allocation Terminal. The Hesper sails herself; Ortiz builds an escort, researches, forms a fleet and fights off two Tide ambushes and Kade's fireship. Vance closes by announcing the Navy will go after the rafts themselves. *Teaches: the shipyard, research, fleets, Escort standing orders, fighting on the move.*
4. **Driftwood** (the Fenwick Isles, June 2061). Vance traces the raiders to Driftwood and orders it burned. Ortiz sails his squadron and a Supply Ship far beyond his supply lines, finds the town, knocks out its platforms and fights its boats on their own raft network. Then he chooses: burn it as ordered, or take it with a marine Beach Landing and detain everyone. Burning pleases Vance and puts people in the water; taking it earns Vance's fury and Lio's promise that Mother Ruiz will hear. *Teaches: scouting, supply lines and the Supply Ship, the Tide, attacking bases, commander powers.*
5. **Reprisal** (the Corrigan Shelf, July 2061). Driftwood brings Mother Ruiz down on the shelf with boats from every raft-town. Admiral Hale takes command of Corrigan Point's defence herself, Ortiz at her side, and orders that nothing fires on a contact nobody has seen. Ruiz's swarm brings refugee boats: families with nowhere else to go, indistinguishable on the scope from gunboats. Hale holds until dawn or hurts Ruiz's Mothership badly enough that she takes the swarm home. If Driftwood was taken, Ruiz offers to turn around in exchange for her people: Hale refuses ("the Accords don't trade") or releases them, which ends the attack, enrages Vance, and puts Hale in the Council's sights. If Driftwood burned, there is no offer; Kade's fireships sail with Ruiz. *Teaches: base defences (Gun Platforms, Radar Masts), repairs (Dry Dock, Damage Control), weather, identifying contacts.*
6. **Clean Sweep** (the Saltpan Channel, September 2061). After Corrigan Point, Hale is called back to headquarters (relieved pending inquiry if she released the detainees, "rested with honours" if she refused) and Vance takes the northern shelves. His orders to Ortiz: clear every raft-town out of the Saltpan Channel so tankers never need an escort again. Ortiz sweeps the coves with Vance's carrier, the *Indomitable*, on loan, while Kade fights back for real and tankers keep sailing the lane. Old Salt Nyx first appears, selling charts of the raft-towns; buy them and Kade keeps turning up wherever the fleet is (Nyx sells to everyone; the payoff comes later). Mercy, the hospital raft, is on Vance's list. Sparing it is never ordered and never rewarded, except quietly. Vance's verdict either way: "Four hundred mouths Ruiz has to feed." *Teaches: carriers, the full economy at scale, assaulting raft-towns, a thinking enemy.*
7. **Bracken** (Bracken Bank, November 2061). The Council (first heard here) raises the northern quotas by eleven percent and orders Bracken Bank drilled, including the rich deposit under Bracken Point's fishing grounds. Ruiz races Ortiz for the bank's oil. The Council orders the village cleared. Ortiz clears it (Bracken burns; Maren's people go to Ruiz) or gives them time (he escorts Maren's three fishing boats out to the west, the Council "notes the delay"). Maren's greeting depends on Mission 2. *Teaches: quotas, racing an expanding enemy for oil, escorting the defenceless.*
8. **The Terminal** (the Allocation Terminal, January 2062). Seen from Vance's chair. Kade throws everything he has at the Terminal, where every barrel on the northern shelves is loaded. He sends the Hesper (Mission 3's tanker), captured, with forty-one of her crew lashed to the rails, rigged to burn. Ortiz begs to go after her. Vance lets him go, or holds the line and sinks her himself. Vance holds until the Home Fleet arrives (Hale with it, if she refused Ruiz at Corrigan Point) or sinks Kade's flagship, the Pyre; Kade escapes either way. *Teaches: a big defence, escort carriers, hard choices from the other chair.*
9. **Open Water** (the Long Reach, March 2062). The battle line meets Ruiz's armada. Vance's carriers hold the line; Ortiz breaks through and sinks Ruiz's three Brood Rafts (the Nest, the Cradle, the Hive), her moving shipyards. Mid-battle, Nyx calls Ortiz privately: Vance has been buying from him for a year (positions, charts, the Saltpan raids), Nyx sold Ortiz's own positions to Kade if he bought the charts, and last week Vance bought the anchorage of Ruiz's home raft, four thousand people. Ortiz tells Vance he knows, or keeps it to himself. *Teaches: fleet battles, fighting beside a scripted ally.*
10. **The Allocation** (the Drowned Reach, May 2062). Haven, Ruiz's home raft (four thousand people on the Motherraft and its four districts), lies in a lagoon behind a ring of reef. Its only ways out are **the Gate** in the west, held by two gun forts and the **Lighthouse**, and **the Needle** to the south-east. Vance's squadron stands off the Gate and leaves the forts to Ortiz: the Council wants to see what he is made of. Kade, with no one left to fight for, raids from the **Cinder Isle** in the north-east. When the Gate falls, the Council speaks for itself for the first time and orders Haven burned. There is one chance to answer. **Obey**: Vance's air wings work Haven over from outside the reef and Ortiz's ships go in; Ruiz is blockaded, Kade comes for the carriers, and the Allocation holds. **Refuse**: Ortiz turns his guns on Vance and Ruiz takes the truce. Vance's second group comes up from the south and the fleet train keeps feeding him ships while a carrier still flies his flag. Haven's boats can only leave through an exit Ortiz has cleared, and they need three thousand people out while Kade's fireships come round the south reef for the districts. What came before comes back: Hale (if she refused Ruiz at Corrigan Point) arrives with the Home Fleet and the Council's inquiry into Vance and Nyx; Maren's boats, Driftwood's people and Mercy's crews help the evacuation if they were spared. Obeying ends with the Council's ribbon. Refusing ends with a charge of mutiny, unless Hale is there to stand Vance down, and a course for open water. *Teaches: everything, at once.*
